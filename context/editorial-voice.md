# VIREZIA — Editorial Voice Guide

**Purpose:** How to write featured property listings, market notes, and Bespoke Living blog posts.
**Status:** Canonical
**Audience:** Lucas, Claude Code agents, future contributors

---

## 1. Core Principle

A VIREZIA featured listing is **not a property description**.
It is **an introduction** — the way an art dealer introduces a painting, the way a magazine opens a profile, the way a curator walks a client through a gallery.

The reader should finish a listing knowing **why this property exists in the world**, not how many bedrooms it has.

Specs are footnotes. Story is the body.

---

## 2. The Listing Architecture

Every featured property follows this structure. Specs and pricing live at the bottom or in a side panel — never in the lead.

### Block 1 — The Lead (1-2 paragraphs)

What makes this property exceptional. The reason it earned a feature.

Hook examples:
- The architect or designer
- The location's lineage (who built here, who came here)
- The story of the property itself (commissioned by, lived in by, restored by)
- The architectural decision that defines it
- The place — what the place gives that nowhere else does

**Bad lead:** "This stunning 4-bedroom oceanfront villa offers panoramic views..."
**Good lead:** "Robert Couturier doesn't build often. When he does, the result is a quiet argument with the rest of the market."

### Block 2 — The Place (2-3 paragraphs)

Where this property exists, in the editorial sense. Not just GPS coordinates — what the place *is*.

- History of the area
- Why this stretch / town / coast / neighborhood matters
- Who else has been drawn here
- The character that makes it worth being there

This is travel-writing-meets-curatorial-essay. It earns the reader's attention before any commercial framing.

### Block 3 — The Property (2-3 paragraphs)

The architecture, the design language, the rooms, the materials, the proportion.

Write it the way an architecture critic would — engaged but precise, observational rather than promotional.

- Specific design choices: material, scale, geometry, light
- What was decided and what was rejected
- How the property sits in the landscape or the urban fabric
- One or two telling details (a roof line, a courtyard, a single window)

### Block 4 — The Practical (compressed, single paragraph or sidebar)

This is the only block where commercial and technical information appears.

- Number of units / size / configuration
- General price range or "available on request"
- Status (under construction / completed / pre-sale)
- Acquisition path (direct, via VIREZIA, by invitation)

Keep it short. Three to five lines maximum.

### Block 5 — The Invitation (1-2 sentences)

How to engage. Always direct, never urgent.

> Express interest in Las Orcas →
>
> Currently accepting expressions of interest from qualified buyers. Inquiries are reviewed personally.

---

## 3. Voice Calibration

### Cadence
- **Slow.** Sentences breathe. Periods are used.
- **Variable rhythm.** Mix long, observational sentences with short, declarative ones.
- **No paragraph longer than 4-5 sentences.** White space is part of the voice.

### Register
- **Confident, not self-aggrandizing.** "We selected this because..." not "We are proud to present..."
- **Observational, not promotional.** Describe what is, not what it makes you feel.
- **Specific, not aspirational.** Name materials, proportions, designers — don't reach for mood-board adjectives.

### Tone benchmarks
Read these for calibration:
- *The New York Times Style Magazine* property profiles
- *Cabana* magazine architectural features
- *Apartamento* magazine interior pieces
- *Monocle* travel and architecture coverage
- A good auction catalog from Christie's or Sotheby's

Avoid:
- Real estate listing copy
- Agency marketing pages
- "Lifestyle" branded content from luxury developers
- Anything written by an AI without curatorial input

---

## 4. Words and Phrases

### Use generously
- Designed, conceived, commissioned, restored
- Sits, occupies, opens onto, faces
- Quiet, considered, deliberate, restrained
- Particular, specific, distinctive
- Material names: limestone, oak, lime plaster, brass, terrazzo
- Architect / designer / artisan names (always, when known)

### Use sparingly (only when literally true)
- Exceptional, rare, unique
- Iconic (almost never — most things are not)
- Modern, contemporary (overused — be more specific)

### Never use
- Stunning, breathtaking, dream
- Indulge, escape, paradise, oasis
- Luxurious, premium, top-tier, world-class
- "Don't miss," "limited opportunity," "must see"
- Generic adjective stacking ("luxurious, exclusive, prestigious")
- Any sentence that could appear unchanged on another agency's site

---

## 5. Length Guidelines

| Format | Word count |
|---|---|
| Featured property listing | 600–1,000 words |
| Market note / regional editorial | 800–1,500 words |
| Bespoke Living blog post | 1,000–2,500 words |
| VIREZIA Circle internal note | No limit — written for one reader |

Shorter is usually better. If the listing can't sustain 600 words of substance, the property may not be ready to feature.

---

## 6. Photography & Visual Pairing

The voice only works with the imagery.

- Architecture-grade photography only. No drone clichés unless the architecture warrants them.
- Negative space matters. White space, empty rooms, single objects.
- Avoid "lifestyle" shots (smiling people, beach scenes, sunset cocktails).
- One hero image. Two to three supporting. Anything more dilutes attention.

If imagery is weak, **the listing is not ready.** A weak photo set undoes 1,000 words of curatorial copy.

## 7. Worked Example (Las Orcas, opening lead)

**Draft 1 — generic luxury voice (rejected):**
> Discover Las Orcas, a stunning new development on the Pacific coast featuring 11 luxurious residences designed by world-renowned architect Robert Couturier. With breathtaking ocean views and unparalleled craftsmanship, these homes redefine coastal living.

**Draft 2 — VIREZIA voice:**
> Robert Couturier rarely works in Mexico. Las Orcas is the exception — eleven residences along a stretch of Pacific coast he chose personally, designed in a register that owes more to mid-century European restraint than to Caribbean resort vocabulary. The houses sit low. They open inward. The architecture argues with itself in places — and that's exactly what makes it good.

The second version says less, signals more, and earns a different kind of reader.

---

## 8. The Curator's Permission

Lucas writes most of these personally. That is the point.

The voice is not a template — it's the result of a particular eye looking at a particular property. Future agents (human or AI) supporting this work should understand: their job is to help **draft and refine**, not to **substitute** for the curatorial reading.

A VIREZIA listing without curatorial judgment is a brochure. The brand collapses without it.
