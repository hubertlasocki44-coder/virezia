# VIREZIA — Brand Guidelines

**Version:** 2.0 (May 2026)
**Status:** Canonical
**Supersedes:** All earlier brand documentation referencing "Bespoke Living" as primary tagline, "Live Beyond Borders" hero, Alexa Ramírez avatar, or "Private Real Estate Intelligence" public-facing language.

---

## 1. What VIREZIA Is

VIREZIA is a **curator-led editorial platform for exceptional homes and real estate investments**.

Each property is selected personally. Each listing is written as editorial. Access is by invitation.

VIREZIA is **not**:
- A marketplace.
- A listing portal.
- A traditional real estate agency.
- A volume play.
- A "real estate intelligence platform" (in public-facing communication).

---

## 2. The Three Brand Pillars

Every public communication must trace back to one or more of these:

### Invitation
You don't browse VIREZIA. You enter through a curated experience — a campaign, a featured property, a referral, an application. Nothing is open by default.

### Exclusivity
Selectivity is the product. VIREZIA features a small number of exceptional properties. The filter is taste, not tier.

### Global Living
Curated worldwide. Currently featuring properties across Latin America, with a focus on Mexico. Welcoming exceptional homes globally — Dubai, Berlin, Lisbon, Buenos Aires, anywhere a property earns the standard.

## 3. Front vs. Backend — The Core Architectural Rule

This is the most important rule in this document.

**Backend (capability — never on public pages):**
- AI tooling, REACH, intelligence systems
- Market analysis, pricing benchmarks, on-site verification
- Due diligence pricing, packages, tiers
- Repositioning frameworks, deal sourcing mechanics
- Internal workflow, automation, agent systems

**Frontend (positioning — what the public sees):**
- Editorial framing of featured properties
- Invitation-only language
- Curator voice
- Story, design, architecture, place
- Apply / Request access CTAs

**Why this rule exists:** Intelligence as a public product reduces VIREZIA to a service provider competing on features. Intelligence as backend creates a moat — competitors see beautiful pages and results but cannot copy the mechanics.

**Test:** If a sentence on a public page describes *how* we work (verification, intelligence, audit pricing), it belongs in backend communication or in post-qualification flow, not on the website.

## 4. Voice & Tone

### Voice characteristics
- **Editorial.** Each property is a story, not a spec sheet.
- **Confident, not loud.** Status comes from restraint.
- **Precise.** Specific over decorative.
- **Quiet.** Zero hype, zero urgency, zero exclamation marks anywhere.
- **Curatorial.** "We selected this because..." — not "Why choose us."

### Tone calibration by context
- **Homepage / Featured properties:** Editorial, slow, declarative.
- **Apply / Forms:** Direct, efficient, respectful.
- **For Owners & Developers:** Selective, professional, slightly demanding.
- **Bespoke Living (blog):** Personal, observational, voice of the curator.
- **VIREZIA Circle:** Minimal. One sentence on public pages. Never describe contents.

---

## 5. Language — Words We Use / Words We Don't

### Use
- *Featured* (the central mechanic)
- *Curated, curatorial, curator*
- *Selected, selection*
- *Editorial*
- *By invitation, by application*
- *Exceptional, unique, distinctive* (only when literally true)
- *Private access*
- *Story, design, architecture, place*
- *Bespoke Living* (blog name only)

### Avoid (front-end)
- *Intelligence, intelligence layer, market intelligence*
- *Verified, audited, due diligence* (in public pricing or copy)
- *Platform, portal, marketplace*
- *Browse, search, explore options, find properties*
- *Listings, inventory*
- *Structured demand*
- *Repositioning* (this is what we sell to owners — not language for buyers)
- *Smart, AI-powered, tech-enabled*
- *Best, top, leading, premier, world-class*

### Never (anywhere)
- Exclamation marks
- "Don't miss out"
- "Limited time"
- Stock-luxury phrases ("indulge yourself," "your dream home awaits")
- Generic urgency
- Adjective stacking ("luxurious, exclusive, premium, prestigious")

## 6. Tagline (LOCKED — May 2026)

**Tagline (H1):**
> Curated real estate, selected — not listed.

**Sub-line / descriptor:**
> A curated real estate network where international investors meet and find good opportunities.

**Settled:**
- "Bespoke Living" = blog name and sub-brand (`/bespoke-living`). Not used as homepage tagline.
- *"Curated"* is the anchor word.
- Tagline + sub-line cover both architectural showpieces and land/investment opportunities under one frame.
- "International investors meet" introduces the network/community dimension — VIREZIA is a connecting layer, not a one-way feed.

**Why these two lines work together:**
- *Tagline* establishes the anti-portal positioning ("selected — not listed").
- *Sub-line* establishes the network function ("where international investors meet and find good opportunities").
- Together they cover both supply and demand framing without using clichés.

**Avoid in tagline (rejected during decision process):**
- *Distinctive properties* — creates unnecessary distance.
- *By invitation* — overused everywhere.
- *Off-market* as categorical claim — not always true.
- *Like art, editorial, gallery* — too narrow for the deal mix.
- *Exceptional, premier, world-class* — luxury cliché.


### Typography
- **Headings:** Cormorant Garamond, weights 300–400, generous letter-spacing
- **Body:** DM Sans, weights 300–400, line-height 1.7
- **Labels:** DM Sans, 12px, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.12em

### Layout principles
- Max content width: 1200px
- Section padding: 120px vertical
- Space is the separator. No decorative dividers, no icon clutter.
- Subtle grain overlay (3% opacity) for texture, not decoration.

### Imagery rules
- No stock photography. Ever.
- No smiling people on beaches.
- No tropical clichés.
- Featured listings: real architecture photography only. If imagery isn't strong, the listing isn't ready.
- Abstract gradient/mesh backgrounds where photography doesn't apply.

## 8. The Selection Standard

What gets featured on VIREZIA:

- Properties that earn a closer look — by architecture, design, location, story, investment thesis, or off-market access.
- Not "luxury by price tier" — *quality of selection*, not tier of price. A $400K mid-century modern qualifies if the design is right. A 36-hectare estate with a strong investment thesis qualifies. A $4M generic beachfront condo doesn't.
- Owners willing to support a proper presentation of the offer.
- Properties Lucas would personally consider featuring if the platform did not exist.

What does not get featured:
- Mass-market product, mass-distributed.
- Anything a buyer could find on a generic portal without context.
- Properties where the owner resists thoughtful presentation.
- Generic "good enough" deals.

**Where featured properties live:** Inside VIREZIA Circle (the internal members-only market). Visible publicly only on dedicated landing pages built per campaign. Never listed on the public homepage.

**Trade-off awareness:** Selectivity costs deals. Refusing a generic offer is a feature of the model, not a sales problem. But selectivity is **broad enough** to include architectural showpieces, design-led residences, distinctive locations, off-market opportunities, and investment-grade assets — not narrow enough to require every property to fit a single aesthetic.

## 9. Anti-Patterns — Things That Erode the Brand

If any of these appear on a public page, the brand has drifted. Reset to this document.

- Pricing tables for services (DD packages, audit tiers, retainers)
- The word *intelligence* in a hero, header, or value prop
- Three-pillar service catalog ("Personal Profile / Curated & Verified / Guided to Close")
- Generic differentiator lists ("Few not many · Verified end-to-end · No hidden fees...")
- Testimonials in standard format
- Case study language that sounds like a B2B SaaS landing page
- Geographic over-promising in the tagline (claiming "worldwide" before delivering it)
- Onboarding copy that reads like a service intake

---

## 10. Decision Filter for Any New Copy

Before any sentence goes on a public VIREZIA page, ask:

1. Does this trace back to **Invitation, Exclusivity, or Global Living**?
2. Is this **editorial** in tone, not service-provider?
3. Does this respect the **front vs. backend** rule?
4. Would a competitor reading this learn how we work? *(If yes — move to backend.)*
5. Could this sentence appear on any luxury real estate site? *(If yes — rewrite or cut.)*

If any answer is no, the copy isn't ready.
